Core Quantum Concepts

Fundamental principles tested in the exam

Quantum Superposition

The fundamental principle of quantum mechanics

Superposition is the ability of a quantum system to exist in multiple states simultaneously until it is measured. Unlike classical objects that must be in one definite state, quantum particles like electrons and photons can be in a combination of states.

Key Insight: A quantum particle can be in state A, state B, or both simultaneously. Measurement collapses this superposition into one definite state.

  • Enables quantum computing with qubits
  • Foundation for quantum interference
  • Critical for quantum algorithms

Quantum Entanglement

"Spooky action at a distance" - Einstein

Entanglement occurs when two or more particles become correlated in such a way that the quantum state of one particle cannot be described independently of the others, even when separated by vast distances.

Key Property: Measuring one entangled particle instantly affects the state of the other, regardless of distance.

  • Enables quantum cryptography
  • Basis for quantum teleportation
  • Powers quantum computing advantage

Photonics & Light

The science of light and photons

Photonics is the technology of generating, controlling, and manipulating light. Photons are the fundamental particles of light and exhibit both wave and particle properties.

Wave-Particle Duality: Light behaves as both a wave (with wavelength and frequency) and a particle (with energy and momentum).

  • Laser technology and applications
  • Optical communication systems
  • Quantum optics experiments

Quantum Electronics

Electron behavior in quantum systems

Quantum electronics studies the behavior of electrons at the quantum level, including phenomena like tunneling, quantized energy levels, and band structure in semiconductors.

Quantum Tunneling: Electrons can pass through energy barriers that would be impossible to cross in classical physics.

  • Semiconductor device physics
  • Transistor and diode operation
  • Quantum well and superlattice structures
